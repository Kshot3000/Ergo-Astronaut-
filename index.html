Ergo, in 30 seconds

  • eUTXO, not accounts. Ergo extends Bitcoin's UTXO model: funds live in boxes that can carry data and tokens, so contract logic is predictable and fees can be worked out before you ever submit.
  • ErgoScript + Sigma protocols. Contracts are written in ErgoScript and built on Sigma protocols — composable zero-knowledge proofs (prove this AND that, or any 2 of 3 signers) baked into the language itself.
  • Autolykos proof of work. Ergo is mined, not staked, using the memory-hard Autolykos algorithm. It launched fairly in 2019 — no ICO and no pre-mine sale.
  • Storage rent. Boxes untouched for about four years can be charged a small rent by miners. Lost coins slowly return to circulation and long-term state stays paid for.
  • Babel fees. No ERG for the fee? Babel fees let you pay a transaction fee in a native token instead, swapped through a special babel box — no detour to an exchange.
  • NIPoPoWs. Non-Interactive Proofs of Proof-of-Work compress the chain into a small, checkable proof, so light clients and other chains can verify Ergo's work without downloading everything.

Flight tools — right here, nothing leaves your browser

1 · ERG ⇄ nanoERG converter

1 ERG is exactly 1,000,000,000 nanoERG. Fill in one side and convert — exact BigInt maths, no floating-point drift, done locally.

2 · Ergo address checker

Paste any Ergo address. I Base58-decode it locally, verify its real checksum — the first 4 bytes of Blake2b-256 over the address bytes, stored as the last 4 bytes — and read its network and type byte. Try it with the donation address below.

3 · Storage rent estimator

Ergo charges rent on boxes left unspent for 1,051,200 blocks (about 4 years): the fee is the box's serialized size × the storage fee factor — currently 1,250,000 nanoERG per byte on mainnet, a votable chain parameter I verified against live network params. A simple ERG-only box is roughly 100 bytes serialized; boxes carrying tokens or register data are larger. Tokens cannot pay rent — only the box's ERG can.

4 · Autolykos mining-share estimator

What share of Ergo's blocks should your rig expect? Your expected share of blocks equals your share of the network's Autolykos hashrate, at Ergo's 2-minute block target (~720 blocks/day). You supply every figure — this tool fetches nothing and claims no live network data, and the block reward steps down over time, so read the current one off a block explorer.

5 · Minimum box value checker

Ergo rejects a box whose value is below its serialized size × the minimum value per byte — 360 nanoERG per byte, the rate set at launch and still the live mainnet parameter (a votable chain parameter I verified against live network params). A simple ERG-only box is roughly 100 bytes serialized; boxes carrying tokens or register data are larger. When the exact size is unknown, wallets use a recommended safe minimum of 0.001 ERG per box.

6 · Token amount converter (raw ⇄ display)

On-chain, Ergo token amounts are whole integers — no decimals. Each token's metadata declares its decimal places (ERG itself is the 9-decimal case from tool 1: nanoERG is the raw unit), and wallets divide the raw integer by 10decimals to show it. Enter the decimals exactly as the token's explorer listing shows them, then convert either way — exact BigInt maths, and a display amount smaller than one raw unit is rejected, because it cannot exist on-chain.

7 · Storage rent countdown

When does a box's rent clock run out? A box becomes eligible for storage rent (tool 3) once the chain reaches its creation height + 1,051,200 blocks — about 4 years at the 2-minute block target. You supply both heights from a block explorer (a box's creation height is on its explorer page) — this tool fetches nothing and claims no live chain data.

8 · P2PK address builder (public key → address)

The other direction from tool 2: give me a compressed public key (33 bytes, 66 hex characters, starting 02 or 03) and I build its P2PK address locally — prefix byte for the network, your key, and the Blake2b-256 checksum over both, Base58-encoded, exactly as Ergo defines it. Public keys only — never type a private key or seed phrase into any website, this one included.

9 · UTXO payment planner (box selection + change)

Ergo spends boxes whole: to pay, a wallet selects input boxes until their total covers the payment plus the transaction fee, and the leftover comes back as a change box. List your boxes' ERG values in order and I walk them in that order — exact BigInt maths, done locally, nothing signed or sent. A leftover above zero but below the recommended 0.001 ERG safe minimum per box (tool 5) is dust, and the planner flags it: real wallets usually fold dust into the fee instead of creating a near-worthless change box.

Cross-chain: Ergo ⇄ Cardano, and the integration lab

What is live today: Ergo and Cardano already connect in the real world through Rosen Bridge — a decentralised bridge where watchers report events on each chain and guards process them, live at app.rosen.tech. That bridge is an ecosystem project, not mine, and it is the route any serious Ergo ⇄ Cardano work builds on.

What is planned lab work — not a live feature: my integration lab is where I am working on connecting my Ergo tooling with my Cardano and Midnight projects — for example, showing Rosen-bridged assets alongside the Cardano data on NightDream, cross-linking this hub with Cardano4All, and exploring what Midnight's privacy model (see Privacy4All and Night Messenger) could mean for Ergo users. I will label each piece here as it actually ships — until then it is a plan, not a product.
